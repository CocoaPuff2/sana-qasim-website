      About

      I know how to make software work. I’m learning how to make it move something.

      I’m Sana Qasim, a software engineer in Seattle. I graduated from the University of Washington with a bachelor’s degree in Computer Science and Software Engineering, and I’ve explored full-stack development, Python, C++, AI, cybersecurity and data analytics.

      Hands-on robotics is new to me. I recently started learning about microcontrollers, sensors, motors, circuits and hardware-software integration, and I’m beginning to experiment with a Raspberry Pi Pico W starter kit. I want to understand how software interacts with real-world systems, not just write code that runs on a computer.

      I’m exploring the field broadly before choosing a specialization. I’m drawn to autonomous vehicles and self-driving cars, home robots, aeronautics, intelligent automated systems, AI applied to real-world problems, and practical uses of technology such as animal welfare. I’m also curious how software, electronics, mechanical systems, sensing and control fit together.

      This site is where I document the transition: what I’m learning, what I’m building, what’s hard, and what I don’t understand yet. I’ll explain concepts so other beginners can follow along, with enough technical depth for experienced engineers, and I’ll include the mistakes and debugging along the way.
